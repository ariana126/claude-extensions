# Appendix B: Monster Lists

## Core Idea
A pure lookup index — Monster Manual creatures organized by environment (10 categories) and challenge rating within each — built specifically to support Chapter 3's random encounter table construction and Chapter 7's treasure-hoard-by-CR placement.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Environment × CR cross-reference**: 10 environment tables — Arctic, Coastal, Desert, Forest, Hill, Mountain, Swamp, Underdark, Underwater, Urban — each listing every terrain-appropriate monster grouped by CR band with its XP value inline. Deliberately omits monsters that don't customarily inhabit an environment (e.g. angels/demons excluded from all of them, since they're planar visitors rather than terrain-native).
  - When to use: exactly the workflow Ch 3 describes for building a Sylvan Forest-style random encounter table — pick an environment, read down the CR column near the party's level, and pull thematically-consistent monsters instead of grabbing names at random.

## Key Concepts
- **Terrain-native vs. planar/imported monsters** — this index only covers creatures that naturally belong to a physical-world environment; extraplanar creatures (fiends, celestials, etc.) are intentionally left out and should be hand-placed for narrative reasons instead.

## Mental Models
- Treat this appendix as the **data source**, not a procedure — the actual "how to build a random encounter table" method lives in Ch 3 ("Creating Random Encounter Tables"); this appendix just supplies the terrain-filtered monster pool to draw from.
- Cross-reference with Ch 7's CR-keyed Treasure Hoard tables when stocking a dungeon (Appendix A) — picking a dominant inhabitant from the matching environment table here, then rolling its hoard from the matching CR band there, keeps a stocked location internally consistent.

## Anti-patterns
- **Pulling monsters into an encounter table without checking environment fit.** The whole point of this appendix is terrain-appropriateness — a random desert table with forest monsters breaks the "establish atmosphere" purpose of random encounters described in Ch 3.

## Worked Example
Stocking a coastal dungeon's dominant inhabitant for a level 9 party (Ch 3's encounter math targets monsters near or somewhat above party level for a memorable single-monster encounter): scan the Coastal Monsters table near CR 8-9, find "Young blue dragon (CR 9, 5,000 XP)" — thematically fits a coastal cliff lair, and its CR feeds directly into Ch 3's XP-threshold math to confirm the fight's difficulty tier for the party.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use this appendix as the terrain-filtered monster pool when building random encounter tables (Ch 3) or stocking dungeon chambers (Appendix A).
2. Extraplanar creatures are intentionally excluded — place them by hand when the story calls for them.
3. Cross-reference a chosen monster's CR against Ch 7's Treasure Hoard tables to keep loot consistent with the encounter.

## Connects To
- **Ch 3**: this appendix is the data source for "Creating Random Encounter Tables" and for picking level-appropriate single monsters.
- **Ch 7**: a monster's CR here directly selects which Treasure Hoard table to roll on.
- **Appendix A**: dominant-inhabitant and random-creature rolls in the dungeon stocking procedure draw from this appendix's environment tables.
