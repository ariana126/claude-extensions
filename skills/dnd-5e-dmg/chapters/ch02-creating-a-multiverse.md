# Chapter 2: Creating a Multiverse

## Core Idea
The multiverse doesn't need to be fully realized — decide only which planes your campaign actually needs (a fiend origin, a celestial origin, an elemental origin, a place for deities, an afterlife, and a way to travel between them), and pick or invent a cosmological *model* (Great Wheel, World Tree, World Axis, or simpler) to organize them.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Minimum viable cosmology**: every campaign needs at minimum (1) a plane of origin for fiends, (2) a plane of origin for celestials, (3) a plane of origin for elementals, (4) a place for deities, (5) a destination for mortal spirits after death, (6) a way to travel between planes, (7) support for Astral/Ethereal-plane spells and monsters. Planes 1-3 and 4-5 can overlap.
  - When to use: before building any planar cosmology, cut scope to only what the campaign needs.
- **Cosmological models** (pick one or invent your own): Great Wheel (concentric rings, Material at center, Outer Planes arranged by alignment, linked by the River Styx) · World Tree (realms on the roots/branches of a cosmic tree, e.g. Yggdrasil) · World Axis (Astral Sea above, Material in the middle, Elemental Chaos + Abyss below) · simpler options: the Omniverse (bare minimum: Material, Transitive, one Elemental Chaos, an Overheaven, an Underworld), Myriad Planes (randomly intersecting bubbles), the Orrery (planes orbit the Material Plane — Eberron's model), the Winding Road (planes as adjacent stops with no deeper cohesion), Mount Olympus / Solar Barge (real-world myth-based single-Material-Plane models), One World (no other planes — everything is a location in the Material Plane), the Otherworld (a single twin-realm reached through "thin places," covers all other-planar roles at once).
  - How: when designing your own plane, give it exactly **one simple, memorable trait** that reflects its philosophy/mood (not just physical description) and is easy to apply at the table.
- **Portal access-requirement types**: Time (opens on a schedule, e.g. full moon, stays open briefly) · Situation (opens under a condition, e.g. rain, a specific spell) · Random (opens for a random duration/traveler count, then shuts) · Command Word (opens on a spoken word) · Key (opens for a bearer of a specific item — Sigil is "City of Doors" because of item-keyed portals). Portals are also typically guarded by a thematically-appropriate guardian creature.
- **Outer Plane optional rules**: each of the 16 Outer Planes has one or more named mechanical "flavor" rules a DM can bolt on to make visiting it feel distinct (see Reference Tables below) — a reusable pattern for homebrew planes: give each one a saving-throw-gated environmental effect tied to its alignment/theme.

## Key Concepts
- **Material Plane** — the "nexus" world where campaigns are set; everything else is defined relative to it.
- **Transitive Planes** — Ethereal and Astral; mostly featureless, exist primarily as travel pathways.
- **Echoes of the Material Plane** — the Feywild (beauty/wonder mirror) and Shadowfell (bleak/twisted mirror), both overlapping the Material Plane 1:1.
- **Inner Planes** — the four Elemental Planes (Air, Earth, Fire, Water) plus the Elemental Chaos that surrounds/dissolves them at their outer extents.
- **Silver cord** — the tether keeping an *astral projection* traveler's consciousness linked to their body; severing it (rare — psychic winds, githyanki silver swords) causes true death.
- **Color pools** — 2D rippling portals in the Astral Plane leading to other planes, identified by a fixed color per destination (see table); finding the right one takes 1d4×10 hours.
- **Fey/Shadow crossings** — natural thin-veil portals between the Material Plane and the Feywild/Shadowfell respectively; open only under specific conditions and can be permanently severed by drastic changes to either side.
- **Domains of Dread** — Shadowfell demiplane-prisons for "darklords" (e.g. Barovia/Strahd), created by the Dark Powers; a template for any DM wanting a pocket-dimension villain lair.
- **Gate-towns** — 16 settlements ringing the Outlands, each built around a permanent portal to one Outer Plane, sharing that plane's flavor.
- **Sigil, City of Doors** — a ring-shaped city atop the Spire at the Outlands' center, riddled with portals, ruled by the enigmatic Lady of Pain; the multiverse's trade hub for portal keys/information.
- **Demiplane** — a self-contained extradimensional space with its own physical laws (spell-created or naturally "pinched off"); can be a single room or an entire realm.
- **Far Realm** — not part of the multiverse proper; a separate reality whose leaking energy warps matter into aberrations (mind flayers, beholders); source of warlock patrons who deal in madness.

## Mental Models
- Treat each plane's "optional rule" as a **one-line environmental hazard/blessing keyed to alignment mismatch** — good creatures suffer on evil planes and vice versa (and lawful/chaotic planes like Mechanus/Limbo are exempt from that specific dissonance). Use this pattern to homebrew new planes fast.
- Think of portal design as a **three-part puzzle**: destination + guardian + access requirement. Any planar quest can be structured as "find the portal → satisfy its requirement → get past its guardian."
- Use **Known Worlds** (Toril/Forgotten Realms, Oerth/Greyhawk, Krynn/Dragonlance, Athas/Dark Sun, Eberron, Aebrynis/Birthright, Mystara) as genre presets — each pairs a Material Plane world with a default Flavor of Fantasy (Ch. 1), so borrowing a world's tone is a shortcut to borrowing its cosmology assumptions too.

## Anti-patterns
- **Building the full published cosmology when the campaign doesn't need it.** The book explicitly frames all >2 dozen planes as optional inventory, not a checklist to complete.
- **Making every plane physically elaborate.** The advice is one simple, evocative trait per plane — over-detailing invites confusion at the table and dilutes the plane's identity.
- **Forgetting that planar relationships are theoretical, not observable.** No being can "see" the Great Wheel's layout; DMs shouldn't treat cosmological diagrams as in-world fact NPCs would agree on.

## Worked Example
A DM wants a planar arc for a Tier 2 party (levels 5-10, per Ch. 1) without committing to the full Great Wheel. They apply the Minimum Viable Cosmology framework: fiends live in a single "Hell" plane (skip the full Nine Hells for now), celestials in a single "Heaven," elementals accessed via one Elemental Plane of Fire (tied to the campaign's volcano dungeon), the dead go to a generic Shadowfell-style gray afterlife, and travel is via a single Command-Word-keyed portal hidden in an ancient temple (satisfying the "way to travel between planes" requirement). For the Fire Plane visit, they borrow the Nine Hells' "Pervasive Evil"-style pattern and invent one rule: "creatures who breathe the ashfall without a *protection from energy* spell must make a CON save each hour or gain a level of exhaustion" — one simple, memorable trait, per the chapter's own guidance for inventing planes.

## Key Takeaways
1. Don't build the whole published multiverse — scope to the 6-7 things a campaign actually needs from its cosmology.
2. Pick one cosmological model (or none) purely as an organizing metaphor; it's never something characters can verify in-world.
3. Give every invented plane exactly one clear, mechanically-expressible trait rather than dense physical description.
4. Use the Time/Situation/Random/Command-Word/Key portal-requirement taxonomy to turn "get to plane X" into a concrete mini-quest.
5. The Outer Planes' optional-rule pattern (alignment-mismatch save-or-shift, or a theme-flavored save-or-effect) is a reusable template for any homebrew plane.
6. Known Worlds (Toril, Oerth, Krynn, Athas, Eberron, Aebrynis, Mystara) double as ready-made Flavor-of-Fantasy + cosmology bundles if you don't want to invent from scratch.
